# Enchantability and Magical Metallurgy

> **Status: highly provisional.** This is a late-game design direction, not part
> of a committed release or current vertical slice.

Metallurgy should continue mattering after the player can mine every ordinary
block. The current direction is to connect a material's magical character to the
enchantments it can support.

## Enchantability as a progression axis

Vanilla enchantability mostly affects the quality of rolls at the enchanting
table. One design candidate makes it a stronger material property: equipment
would have an enchantability tier that limits the maximum enchantment levels or
rarities it can hold.

Under that model:

- Powerful base materials are not automatically the best magical vessels.
- Rare enchantments may require equipment prepared for high-tier magic.
- The enchanting table, anvil, loot, and repair systems must agree on the same
  rules.
- Material choice remains relevant after raw harvesting power is solved.

The number of tiers, exact limits, treatment of existing items, and whether
Mending belongs at a high tier are all undecided.

## Candidate material identities

The current fantasy uses several related materials:

- **Copper** is an accessible base with long-term value through alloys.
- **Gold** is the magical metal: soft or impractical in mundane use, but
  exceptionally receptive to enchantment.
- **Silver** is a perfecting or finishing metal rather than merely another tool
  tier.
- **Rose gold** combines copper's metallurgical foundation with gold's magical
  potential.
- **Silvered rose gold** represents an artifact-grade enchanting vessel.
- **Netherite** remains the intentionally excessive raw-power capstone.

These identities are design candidates. Silvering netherite may be unnecessary
precisely because netherite already occupies the extreme end of material power.

## Why rose gold is a useful poster child

Rose gold expresses the entire intended system inside one material path:

1. An accessible early resource remains relevant.
2. Alloying combines properties rather than only increasing a number.
3. A precious metal connects metallurgy to magic.
4. A finishing material extends the path into artifact crafting.
5. The result enables something that no ingredient could do alone.

The exact recipe, processes, experience cost, lapis use, scribing mechanics,
enchantment ceilings, and final item properties are not decided.

## Design constraints

Any enchantability system must:

- Be understandable without requiring external spreadsheets.
- Apply consistently across enchanting, anvils, books, loot, commands, and
  compatibility paths.
- Avoid turning every non-magical material into a trap.
- Preserve meaningful reasons to use both raw-power and magical-specialist gear.
- Create new decisions rather than an obligatory chain of upgrades.
- Justify every added finishing or scribing step with a distinct result.

This system may eventually belong in a separate compatible module if keeping
physical progression and magical progression separable produces a better mod.
